01
Yummies Fast Food & Restaurant — Supervisor

Rebuilding revenue after a decline

The restaurant had previously run at ₦1,000,000 a day in sales, but revenue had fallen off after a change in management — new supervisors weren't holding the standard, and daily sales had dropped to around ₦300,000 by the time I stepped in.

What I changed

We brought in new equipment and hired a dedicated baker to strengthen the bakery side of the business, which had been underused. To drive traffic, I helped launch a 15% discount promotion targeted at a university near the restaurant — a customer base we weren't capturing. On the delivery side, we added an extra dispatch bike and split coverage between in-store and online orders, which cut delivery times noticeably.

On the floor, I held the team of 17 to fast-food service standards — punctuality, professionalism, and no tolerance for the discipline lapses that had crept in under the previous supervisors.

Result

Revenue climbed in stages — ₦300K to ₦500K, then ₦700K, before returning to the ₦1,000,000-a-day mark the restaurant had hit previously. Service got faster, deliveries got faster, and the team ran with more discipline than before.

02
The Rock Hospital — Accountant & Administrative Officer II

Clearing an HMO debt backlog

Before I joined, a backlog of unsent HMO bills had put the hospital in a debt position — claims simply weren't going out, and the hospital's own pricing on services and drugs was under market rate.

What I changed

I worked through the backlog, submitting outstanding bills both on HMO portals and directly by email, and followed up with each HMO individually to get them paid. Alongside that, I negotiated higher pricing on services and drugs to bring the hospital closer to fair market rates. I also kept staff payroll running smoothly and reorganized the hospital's email and documentation backlog so nothing else slipped through.

The billing backlog was resolved, pricing was corrected, and the hospital successfully onboarded Leadway Health as a new HMO partner during my time there — expanding the hospital's patient coverage options.

03
Nigeria Customs Service (NYSC) — Administrative Assistant

From documentation to verification

Days ran from 8:30am to 9:30pm, driven by the schedule of vehicles coming through for clearance. The core of the job was using both legacy and newer verification software to check documents submitted by clearing agents against what was actually being declared.

Result

I was promoted into the verification office — a more sensitive role focused specifically on identifying illegal documentation and falsified duty payments before they cleared. It's the role I'm proudest of from this position: it meant being trusted with the part of the process that actually catches fraud, not just processes paperwork.

—
What ties these together

Different industries, same underlying job: find what isn't being tracked or enforced, and rebuild the process around it — whether that's a discount promo and stricter floor standards, chasing down unpaid HMO claims, or catching a falsified document before it clears.
